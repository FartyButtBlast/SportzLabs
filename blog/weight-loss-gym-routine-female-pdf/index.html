Weight-Loss Gym Routine for Women: A Printable Beginner Plan

Download a simple strength-training routine and learn how to choose weights, record your sessions and adapt the plan over four weeks.

By SportzLabs · 10 November 2026

About a 5-minute read

A printable gym routine can remove one of the biggest obstacles to starting: deciding what to do when you arrive. But a list of exercises only becomes useful when you know how hard to work, when to rest and what to change next time. This guide explains a straightforward beginner plan and includes a downloadable workout sheet you can take to the gym.

The routine is intended for adult women starting resistance exercise who also have a weight-loss goal. It is a general example, not an individual prescription. Use it to organise your first month, and ask a qualified gym instructor to check unfamiliar movements before increasing the weights.

Download your workout sheet

Download the printable beginner strength routine (PDF). It includes two sessions, repetition targets, a four-week planning grid and space to record your training. You can use paper at the gym or enter the same exercises in Workout Studio.

Read the instructions below before treating the sheet as a checklist. Completing every box is not the goal if you are tired, uncomfortable or still learning an exercise. The best version of the plan is one you can perform with control and adapt to your circumstances.

Understand what the plan can do

Resistance training can support a weight-loss programme by helping you retain fat-free mass. A 2025 review found that adding resistance exercise to dietary weight loss improved fat loss and protected fat-free mass compared with dietary weight loss alone, without a significant additional effect on total body weight. (Binmahfoz A, Dighriri A, Gray C, Gray SR, 2025)

That finding explains why this sheet tracks performance as well as attendance. A smaller scale reading is not the only useful outcome. The research does not prove that this exact exercise list will produce a certain result, and it does not justify a rapid-weight-loss promise. Food intake and other individual factors remain part of the picture.

Use the two-day version first

Schedule Session A and Session B on non-consecutive days, such as Monday and Thursday. Start with one working set per exercise while learning the equipment, then use two sets when comfortable. Allow five to ten minutes for easy movement beforehand, plus a lighter practice set for loaded exercises.

Session A contains leg press, seated cable row and machine chest press for eight to twelve repetitions each; glute bridge for ten to fifteen; and dead bug for five to eight per side. Session B contains goblet squat to a bench or leg press, lat pulldown, and incline push-up or machine chest press for eight to twelve; seated leg curl for ten to fifteen; and supported split squat for six to eight per side.

Use the split squat only if you can keep your balance and move comfortably. Ask an instructor for a familiar machine alternative if needed. The PDF leaves room to write your chosen alternative so that next week's session does not become another exercise-selection problem.

Fill in the sheet before and after lifting

Before the first working set, write the exercise variation and planned load. After the set, write the repetitions you actually completed. A target of twelve is not the same as completing twelve, so avoid copying the target into every box automatically. If you changed equipment, record that too.

For dumbbells, decide whether you are recording each dumbbell or their combined weight and keep that convention. For machines, include the machine name or location when two machines feel different. For bodyweight movements, record the support height or exercise version. These small details make the log more useful than a bare number.

Choose an appropriate starting effort

The early sets should feel controlled, with approximately two or three good repetitions left when you stop. You do not need to test the heaviest weight you can lift. If you have to twist, bounce or shorten the movement unexpectedly to reach the target, the load or exercise version needs adjusting.

Rest around two minutes between working sets and longer if needed. Keep enough time in the session to practise rather than rush. If the gym is busy, use the alternative you agreed with an instructor, or return to that exercise later. There is no special benefit to following the printed order at the expense of a calm, manageable visit.

Follow a simple four-week progression

Week one is an introduction: learn the settings and choose conservative loads. In week two, aim to repeat the movements more confidently and use two sets if recovery is comfortable. In week three, add a repetition where you can do so without losing control. In week four, review the log before deciding whether any load should increase.

Progressive training involves adjusting the challenge as capacity improves; the ACSM position stand provides a framework for this approach. (American College of Sports Medicine, 2009) For this starter plan, try a small load increase only after reaching the top of the range in every set on two visits. You can stay at the same load longer when needed.

Review the plan rather than judging yourself

At the end of each week, ask three questions: Did I attend? Did the movements feel clearer? Did I recover well enough to train again? If the answer to the last question is no, reduce sets or allow more recovery. An unfinished sheet can contain useful information about what needs to change.

Research in healthy adult women supports resistance training for strength and muscle development. (Hagstrom AD, Marshall PWM, Halaki M, Hackett DA, 2020) It does not require your first month to look like an experienced lifter's schedule. Keep the plan deliberately small while you learn what fits your life. Fat loss, if desired, should be considered alongside sustainable nutrition and realistic expectations.

Keep a longer record in Workout Studio

A printed sheet is convenient for a session; Workout Studio can keep your templates and workout history together over time. Copy your chosen exercise versions into A and B templates and log completed sets, repetitions and weights. Review previous workouts before deciding what to attempt next.

Use either paper or the app as your main record so you do not create extra administration. Neither can assess pain or technique. Stop for sharp pain or unusual symptoms, and seek individual guidance for pregnancy, postpartum recovery, injury or a condition affecting exercise. The sheet is a starting tool that should serve you, not a set of instructions to push through regardless.
