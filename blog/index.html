TRAINING GUIDE · 2 OCTOBER 2026

Strength training for cyclists

A stronger cyclist is not necessarily the cyclist who lifts the most. The goal is to produce useful power, hold your position under load and finish key efforts without taking away from the rides that matter.

Why strength training belongs in a cycling plan

Cycling is excellent endurance training, but it does not expose your body to every kind of strength demand. Progressive resistance work can help you produce force at the pedals, maintain position under fatigue and handle short, hard efforts when the road rises or the pace changes.

What to train

Choose movements you can perform well, load progressively and recover from. A simple full-body session can cover knee-dominant work such as squats or step-ups, hip-dominant work such as Romanian deadlifts, single-leg control, upper-body pushing and pulling, and core or lower-leg capacity.

How often should cyclists lift?

Two non-consecutive, full-body sessions per week is a practical starting point for many riders. Protect the cycling sessions closest to your goal and progress one variable at a time: load, repetitions, range of motion or a more demanding variation.

The takeaway

Strength training can make cycling training more complete. Begin with manageable movements, progress gradually, keep recovery visible and judge success by the whole week—not by a single gym session.

TRAINING GUIDE · 9 OCTOBER 2026

How to build a workout logging habit

A workout log becomes useful when it is easy enough to keep. Start with the smallest record that helps you make a better next decision.

Record the plan before you start

Write down the exercises, sets or time you intend to complete. This gives the session a shape and makes it easier to tell the difference between the plan and what actually happened.

Capture completed work, not perfect work

Log the sets, reps, weight, time or distance you completed. If a set is shortened or skipped, leave a note rather than rewriting the plan. Honest records are more useful than impressive ones.

Keep the next step visible

After the session, look for one small signal: an extra rep, a steadier pace, a more comfortable movement or simply another completed session. That signal can guide the next workout without demanding a complicated analysis.

Make consistency easier with a reusable routine

A reusable template removes the friction of deciding what to do each time. Workout Studio lets you build routines for strength, cardio and mobility, then review the sessions you have completed.

PRODUCT UPDATE · 14 OCTOBER 2026

A clearer way to track workouts

Workout Studio keeps the useful detail together across strength, cardio and mobility sessions.

Log what you can act on

Record the exercise, sets, reps and weight for strength work; duration and distance for cardio; and time or completed sets for mobility. The aim is a useful record, not paperwork.

Keep routines reusable

Build a routine once, then return to it for the next session. A consistent structure makes it easier to compare what you planned with what you completed.
