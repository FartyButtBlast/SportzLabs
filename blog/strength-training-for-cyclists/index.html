Strength training for cyclists

How to add progressive strength work to your riding week, what the research supports and how to protect recovery.

By SportzLabs · 2 October 2026

Why strength training belongs in a cycling plan

Cycling is excellent endurance training, but it does not expose your body to every kind of strength demand. Progressive resistance work can help you produce force at the pedals, maintain position under fatigue and handle short, hard efforts when the road rises or the pace changes.

The research is encouraging, with an important qualification. A recent systematic review and meta-analysis of 17 controlled studies involving 262 endurance cyclists found that heavy strength training improved pooled measures of cycling performance, cycling efficiency and anaerobic power—qualities that can matter during climbs, accelerations and hard finishes. It did not find significant pooled improvements in VO2max, power at VO2max, maximal metabolic steady state or anaerobic capacity, and the authors rated the certainty of the evidence as low. In other words, strength work is a useful performance tool—not a guaranteed shortcut to a higher FTP.

What to train

You do not need a sacred list of cyclist exercises. Choose movements you can perform well, load progressively and recover from. A simple full-body session can cover these patterns:

Knee-dominant

Goblet squats, split squats, step-ups or a leg press. These train the quads and glutes through a controlled range.
